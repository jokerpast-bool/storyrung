CHAPTER 1. Sammy Jay Makes a Fuss（1）

*I*

*SAMMY JAY MAKES A FUSS*

Sammy Jay does not mind the cold of winter. In fact, he likes it. Under his handsome blue coat with white edges, he wears a warm, soft suit of underclothes. He laughs at rough Brother North Wind and his cousin, Jack Frost. But he does not like winter as much as the warmer seasons. Why? Well, because he is a lazy fellow. He does not like to work hard for a living. In winter it is not so easy to find something to eat.

There is another reason why Sammy Jay does not like winter as much as the other seasons. What do you think it is? It is not a good reason at all. No, Sir, it is not a good reason at all. It is because it is not so easy to make trouble. Somehow, Sammy Jay never seems really happy unless he is making trouble for someone else. He just loves to bother other little people of the Green Meadows and the Green Forest.

Dear, dear, it is a terrible thing to say, but Sammy Jay is bold and bad. He steals! Yes, Sir, Sammy Jay steals whenever he gets a chance. He would rather steal a breakfast any time than get it in an honest way. Now people who steal usually are very clever and secret. Sammy Jay is clever and secret. In fact, he is one of the most clever and secret of all the little people who live in the Green Forest. He does not spend his time honestly looking for his meals. Instead, he spends most of it watching his neighbors to find out where they keep their food. Then he can help himself when they are not looking. He moves through the Green Forest as quietly as can be. He hides in the thick tree-tops and behind the trunks of big trees. He looks out with those sharp eyes of his at his neighbors. Whenever someone sees him, he always pretends to be very busy with his own business. He also pretends to be very surprised to find anyone is near.

It was in this way that he had found one of the food stores of Chatterer the Red Squirrel. He did not let Chatterer know that he had found it. Oh, my, no! He did not even go near it again for a long time. But he did not forget it. Sammy Jay never forgets things of that kind, never! He thought of it often and often. When he did, he would say to himself:

"Sometime when the snow is deep And Chatterer is fast asleep, When Mother Nature is unkind And things to eat are hard to find, I'll help myself and fly away To steal again some other day."

The snow was deep now, and things to eat were hard to find. But Chatterer the Red Squirrel was not asleep. No, indeed! Chatterer seemed to like the cold weather. He was as full of life and quick as ever. And he never went very far away from that food store. Sammy Jay watched and watched. But he never once got a chance to steal the sweet acorns that he had seen Chatterer store away in the fall.

"H-m-m!" said Sammy Jay to himself. "I must do something to get Chatterer away from his food store."

For a long time Sammy Jay sat in the top of a tall, dark pine-tree. He thought and thought. Then his sharp eyes shone with a look of great cleverness, and he laughed quietly. It was a bad laugh. Away he flew to a very thick spruce-tree some distance away in the Green Forest. But he went where Chatterer the Red Squirrel could hear him. There Sammy Jay began to make a great noise. He screamed and cried out as only he can. Pretty soon, just as he expected, he saw Chatterer the Red Squirrel hurry over to see what the noise was all about. Sammy Jay slipped out of the other side of the spruce-tree. Without a sound he hurried over to Chatterer's food store.

*II*

*A BITTER DISAPPOINTMENT*

As he flew through the Green Forest, Sammy Jay laughed and laughed to himself. It was not a good laugh to hear. It was the kind of laugh that only folks who are doing wrong use. They think they are smart because they are doing wrong. Sammy Jay thought that he was smart, very smart indeed. He had screamed and cried out and made a great noise over nothing at all. He did this until Chatterer the Red Squirrel had hurried over to find out what it all meant. Then Sammy Jay had slipped away without being seen. He came straight to the food store of Chatterer the Red Squirrel.

This particular food store had once been the home of Blacky the Crow. When Blacky left it for a new home, Chatterer had taken it for a food store. He had put a roof over it. All through the pleasant fall he had stored away nuts and acorns in it. Sammy Jay had watched him. He had seen those sweet acorns and nuts put there. He had never forgotten them. Now, with the snow deep on the ground, the easiest way to get a good meal that he knew of was to steal some of those very acorns. So he laughed as he pulled apart the roof of Chatterer's food store in search of those acorns.

Now Chatterer the Red Squirrel is just as smart as Sammy Jay. In fact, he is very much like Sammy Jay. He is a trouble-maker and a stealer himself. People who do wrong always watch for others to do wrong. So Chatterer the Red Squirrel had kept his sharp eyes wide open the whole time he was filling his store-house in the fall. He had seen Sammy Jay's smart blue coat when Sammy thought he was well hidden. Chatterer had known why Sammy Jay was hiding there. His sharp eyes flashed. But he went right on filling his store-house just the same. Then, as soon as he was sure that Sammy Jay had gone away, Chatterer had taken out every one of the sweet nuts and put them in another store-house inside a tree with an empty middle. He had left nothing but hickory nuts. He knew that these are too hard for Sammy Jay to break open.

But Sammy Jay did not know anything about this. So now, as he broke his way into the store-house, he laughed low and long with greed. Pretty soon he had a hole big enough to put his head in. His mouth watered as he reached in for a sweet nut. All he could find were hard hickory nuts. What did it mean? In a great rage, Sammy Jay began to tear the store-house to pieces. There must be some sweet nuts there somewhere! Had he not seen Chatterer put them there? He forgot that he was stealing. He forgot everything except his disappointment. The more he thought of this, the angrier he grew. He would have pulled the store-house all to pieces, if Chatterer himself had not come home.

Sammy Jay had just stopped for breath when he heard the sound of claws on the bark of the tree. He knew what that meant. He did not wait to look down. He just spread his blue wings and with a scream of rage flew over to the next tree. Then there was such a terrible noise in the Green Forest!

"Robber!" screamed Chatterer the Red Squirrel, dancing up and down with anger.

"Stealer yourself!" screamed Sammy Jay.

It was a terrible fight with words, and all the little forest people who were near enough to hear stopped their ears.

*III*

*THE VANITY OF SAMMY JAY*

When Sammy Jay is not planning trouble, or putting his bill into the affairs of other folks that are none of his concern, or trying to make some one bigger than himself afraid or make some one smaller than himself afraid, he spends a great deal of his time admiring his fine clothes and thinking what a handsome fellow he is. And he is a handsome fellow. Even Chatterer the Red Squirrel, who is always fighting with words with him, admits that Sammy Jay is a handsome fellow. He carries himself proudly when he thinks any one is looking. His shape is very neat and tidy, and he is a very smart looking fellow indeed. And his coat! Was there ever such a coat before? It seems as if Old Mother Nature must have cut off a little piece of the sky when it was bluest on a summer day to make Sammy Jay's coat. And she must have taken a tiny strip from the whitest cloud to decorate it with. And then she gave him a smart cap and a black collar and a waistcoat of just the softest grayish-white, that shows off his blue coat best. Old Mother Nature certainly was feeling very good indeed when she planned Sammy Jay's clothes.

Now Sammy Jay knows just how handsome he is. If you should ask him, and he would lower himself to talk to you at all, which he probably would not do, he would tell you that he is the handsomest fellow in the world. Of course this is not true, but Sammy Jay thinks it is. And so Sammy Jay is very fond of showing off his fine clothes and making fun of other people who are not so finely dressed. He spends a great deal of time in caring for his beautiful coat and in admiring himself whenever he can see his reflection in a little pool of water.

Now Peter Rabbit is not the least bit like Sammy Jay. He does not think about his clothes at all. Indeed, Peter thinks so little about his clothes that it does not trouble him a bit to wear a white patch on the seat of his trousers. And Peter loves very much to make fun of Sammy Jay.

So Peter was very much pleased one day to find Sammy Jay admiring himself. Peter had come up through the Green Forest without making a sound. The snow covered the ground, so there were no dead leaves to rustle. As usual, his long ears were up to catch every sound. Suddenly Peter stopped. He had heard Sammy Jay's voice. By the sound, Peter knew that Sammy was talking to himself. Very, very softly Peter moved forward and hid where he could see Sammy Jay in a big pine-tree.

"I've got the handsomest coat in all the Green Forest!" said Sammy Jay. He stretched one of his wings out and turned his head to one side to admire it. "And where else is such a beautiful tail to be found?" He spread his tail so that a ray of sunshine would fall on it. It certainly was very beautiful. It was as blue as the sky, with a little band of white across the tip and little bars of black across the outer sides. Even Peter Rabbit, with his nose turned up in scorn, had to admit to himself that it certainly was a handsome tail.

"I'm so glad it's mine!" sighed Sammy Jay. "It must be terrible not to be handsome."

Peter Rabbit could keep still no longer. "It's a good thing you admire yourself, Sammy Jay, because no one else does!" he shouted.

"Handsome is as it may do! Don't forget that, Sammy Jay. Underneath that coat of blue Is a black heart, Sammy Jay. Everybody near and far Knows you for just what you are-- Of all trouble-makers chief. Handsome clothes won't hide a stealer."

Sammy Jay flew into a rage. But when he opened his mouth to call Peter names, all he could say was "Stealer! Stealer! Stealer!"

"What did I tell you?" said Peter Rabbit, grinning.

*IV*

*SAMMY JAY GETS EVEN WITH PETER RABBIT*

"I'll get even with you, Peter Rabbit! I'll get even with you!" Sammy Jay jumped up and down on the branch of the big pine. He was so angry. Peter just put his tongue in one cheek in the most rude way and then laughed at Sammy Jay. Of course it is true, as every one in the Green Forest and on the Green Meadows knows, that Sammy Jay is a robber. But no one likes to be told that he is a robber, even if he is, Sammy Jay least of all. Like many other people who do wrong, Sammy Jay likes to pretend that he is a very fine gentleman. He wants other people to think so too. So he takes great care of his handsome blue coat and walks around proudly a great deal when he thinks other folks are looking at him.

So Sammy Jay thought and thought how he could get even with Peter Rabbit. He called Peter bad names whenever he saw him. But Peter did not mind that at all, for he could call names back again. Besides, names never hurt, and it is very silly to call them. So Sammy Jay thought and thought how he could get even with Peter Rabbit in some other way. Then one day, as he sat in the big pine-tree thinking, Sammy heard a voice that gave him an idea. It was the voice of Redtail the Hawk, who, you know, is own cousin to old Whitetail and to Roughleg. Now Sammy Jay can scream so exactly like Redtail the Hawk that you cannot tell their voices apart. When he heard that scream, Sammy Jay laughed out loud. He had thought of a plan to get even with Peter Rabbit.

Every day after that, Sammy Jay went looking and searching through the Green Forest and around the edge of the Green Meadows without making a sound. He was just watching for Peter Rabbit. The snow was almost all gone. That is how it happened that Redtail had come back from the South where he had spent the winter. Sammy Jay felt quite sure that Peter did not know that Redtail was back yet. He hoped he did not, anyway.

Early one morning, Sammy Jay sat hidden on the edge of the Green Forest. He was watching the Old Briar-patch where Peter Rabbit lives. He saw Peter come out of one of his private little paths and sit up very straight. For a long time Peter sat looking this way and looking that way over the Green Meadows. He was sure that Reddy and Granny Fox were nowhere about. He was sure that Roughleg was nowhere in sight. Then Peter kicked up his heels and ran out on to the Green Meadows away from the dear Old Briar-patch. He wanted to see if there were any signs of spring.

Sammy waited until Peter had reached the big hickory-tree over by the Smiling Pool. Then very quietly he flew over to the big hickory-tree. Peter was so busy looking for Jerry Muskrat that he did not see Sammy Jay at all. Suddenly, right over Peter's head, sounded a fierce, high scream. Peter knew that voice. At least, he thought he did. He did not stop to look. He had learned long ago that it is best to run first and look afterward. So now he started for the dear Old Briar-patch as fast as his long legs would take him. His heart was in his mouth.

Again that fierce scream sounded right over him. Peter ran faster than ever. As he ran, he turned this way and turned that way. Every second he expected to feel the sharp nails of Redtail the Hawk. My, such jumps as Peter did take! It seemed to him that he never would reach the dear Old Briar-patch. But he did. Just as soon as he was safely inside, he turned around to see what had become of Redtail. And what do you think he saw? Why, only Sammy Jay laughing fit to kill himself.

"Fraidcat! Fraidcat!" shouted Sammy Jay.

Peter shook his fist. Then he grinned sillily. "I guess you are even, Sammy Jay!" he said.

Peter Rabbit had a very strange feeling. He had started out that morning with the best intentions in the world. He had meant to go straight to Chatterer the Red Squirrel. He wanted to tell him how mean he had been to watch and so find the new house that Chatterer was trying to keep a secret. Then he had heard Chatterer telling Tommy Tit the Chickadee how he had fooled Peter. Chatterer said that Peter did not know where the new house was, at all. Peter had never felt more silly in his life. No, Sir, he never had felt more silly in his life. Of course, it was true that he had been fooled. He really did not know where Chatterer's new house was. So there was no use in asking Chatterer's forgiveness. He would only make himself still more of a laughing stock than he was already. And yet the thing he had done was just as mean as if he had found out Chatterer's secret. He knew that he would feel better if he owned up. He rubbed his left ear with his right back foot. Then he rubbed his right ear with his left back foot. He pulled his face hairs, and still he did not know what to do.

He was still trying to decide when he heard a great noise from the direction of the Green Forest. It was Sammy Jay, screaming loudly as usual. He was hurrying straight up to the Old Orchard. Of course Chatterer heard him. As soon as Sammy was near enough to hear, he called to him. Sammy hurried over at once.

"So here you are!" he said loudly. "I've hunted all through the Green Forest for you until I'm quite tired out. I've got news for you."

"What is it?" begged Chatterer, dancing about with no patience.

"I've seen Shadow the Weasel," replied Sammy.

"Where is he?" asked Chatterer, and his voice sounded very worried.

"He's over in the Green Forest. He says he is going to stay there until he catches you, if he has to stay all winter," replied Sammy. "He says he is going to find you if he has to hunt through every tree in the Green Forest."

Chatterer actually turned pale for a minute. "You--you did not tell him that I was not in the Green Forest, did you?" he asked.

"Of course I did not! How could I when I did not know it myself?" answered Sammy with scorn.

"And--and you will not tell him when you see him again, will you, Sammy?" begged Chatterer.

"What do you take me for?" asked Sammy in an angry way. "I have no love for you, Chatterer, and you know it. You are a red-headed, red-coated problem, and I am not at all sorry to see you in trouble. But I would not give my worst enemy to such a mean, cold-hearted robber as Shadow the Weasel. He would kill me as quickly as he would you, if he could catch me. He cannot catch me. I will make it my business to see that all the little people who are afraid of him know that he is around. I am going over to the Old Briar-patch right away to warn Peter Rabbit."

"You do not need to, because I am right here," said Peter from his hiding place. "I am very thankful to you for planning to warn me. I am sorry I have ever said unkind things about you, Sammy Jay."

"Pooh!" replied Sammy. "You do not need to be. I guess I have deserved them."

Then Sammy and Peter and Chatterer began to talk over the news about Shadow the Weasel with such strong interest that not one of them saw Black Pussy stealing along the old stone wall.

CHAPTER 2. Sammy Jay Makes a Fuss（2）

*VI*

*BLACK PUSSY ALMOST CATCHES A GOOD BREAKFAST*

Black Pussy was out very early, hunting for her breakfast. She did not need to hunt for her breakfast; oh, my, no! Black Pussy needed nothing at all. Every morning Farmer Brown's boy filled a small dish with warm fresh milk for her. Every day she had all the meat that was good for her. So there was no need at all for her to go hunting. Black Pussy was just like all cats. She lay before the fire in Farmer Brown's house, blinking and making a soft sound of happiness. She seemed too gentle and kind to hurt anyone. All Farmer Brown's family said she was gentle, and they believed it. They knew nothing about the empty little nests in the happy spring. Those nests were empty because Black Pussy had found them and emptied them. She had broken the hearts of little father and mother birds.

You see, Farmer Brown's family really did not know Black Pussy. But the little forest and field people did. They knew that Black Pussy was just like all cats: fierce and hard inside. They all hated Black Pussy, every one of them. They knew that deep in her heart was the love of killing. It was the same love of killing that is in the heart of Shadow the Weasel. So they hated Black Pussy. If she had had to hunt for a living, they would not have minded so much. But she did not have to hunt for a living, and so they hated her.

This morning Black Pussy had chosen to look along the old stone wall at the edge of the Old Orchard. Many times she had hunted Striped Chipmunk there. She did not know enough about the ways of the little people of the Green Forest and the Green Meadows. She did not know that this cold weather had sent Striped Chipmunk down into his warm bedroom under ground for a long sleep. So she moved quietly along from stone to stone, hoping to surprise him. She had gone half the length of the old wall without a sign of anything to catch. Then she heard voices. They put all thought of Striped Chipmunk out of her head. She crawled flat on her stomach to keep out of sight. She moved softly nearer and nearer. Then she looked from behind a big stone in the old wall. She could see Chatterer the Red Squirrel, Peter Rabbit, and Sammy Jay. They were talking so busily and were so excited that they did not seem to pay attention to anything else.

Sammy Jay was safe, because he was sitting in an old apple tree. But Chatterer was on the old wall, and Peter was on the ground. Which should she catch? Peter would make the biggest and best breakfast. But Black Pussy had not forgotten the terrible kick he had once given her when she had caught little Miss Fuzzytail up in the Old Pasture. She had great respect for Peter's strong back legs. She would be happy to catch Chatterer this morning. She hated him anyway, for he had been very rude to her many times. He would never make fun of her or call her names again.

More slowly and carefully than ever, Black Pussy moved forward. Her eyes grew yellow with excitement, and fierce and hard. At last she reached a place where one good jump would land her on Chatterer. Carefully she drew her feet under her to make the jump. The end of her black tail moved with eagerness. Just as she got ready to spring, there was a sharp scream from Sammy Jay. He had caught sight of the moving tip of that tail, and he knew what it meant. Black Pussy sprang, but she was just too late. Chatterer had gone down headfirst between the stones of the old wall at the sound of Sammy's scream. Peter had gone headfirst into Johnny Chuck's house. He happened to be sitting on the doorstep of that house. Black Pussy looked up at Sammy Jay and snarled at him in a terrible rage. Sammy shrieked at her just as angrily. Then, when her head was turned for just an instant, he darted down. He actually pulled a tuft of hair from her coat. He was safely out of the way before she could turn and spring. Then Black Pussy thrust a paw down between the stones where Chatterer had disappeared. She pulled it out again with a yowl of pain, for sharp little teeth had bitten it. Slowly and sullenly Black Pussy turned and limped back towards Farmer Brown's house. She suddenly remembered that dish of milk. That was really all the breakfast she wanted.

*VII*

*CHATTERER WORKS HARD*

When Chatterer had left the Green Forest because of his terrible fear of Shadow the Weasel, he had been fat. At least, he had been fat for him. All through the pleasant fall, he had been gathering his supply of nuts and seeds to store away for the winter. He had eaten all he could hold. He had filled his red coat out until it actually felt too tight. But now that same red coat hung so loose on Chatterer that it looked too big for him. Yes, Sir, Chatterer had grown so thin that his coat actually looked too big for him. And he was growing thinner every day.

You see, most of the food had been collected and stored away long ago. Chatterer had to run about a great deal and hunt very hard to find enough to eat day by day. As for filling a new store-house, that seemed impossible! Still Chatterer kept trying. Day by day he managed to add a little to the supply of seeds. But it was pretty poor food at best. There were no plump nuts or tasty pine seeds, such as filled his store-houses in the Green Forest. No nut or pine trees grew near the Old Orchard. Chatterer did not dare go back to the Green Forest for fear that Shadow the Weasel would find him and track him to his new home. So he patiently did his best to find food close at hand. But it was discouraging, terribly discouraging. He worked from sun-up to sun-down, running here, running there, running everywhere. He was so tired he was ready to drop. And he knew all the time that the snow might come any day and bury what little food there was. Oh, those were hard days for Chatterer the Red Squirrel, very hard days indeed.

One morning he started very early. He made a long journey by way of the old stone wall and the rail fences down to Farmer Brown's cornfield. Of course Farmer Brown had taken away the corn long ago. But while doing it, many grains had been scattered on the ground. Some were half buried where they had been stepped on. Some were hidden under leaves and among weeds and under the piles of stalks from which the ears had been stripped. For the first time in days Chatterer felt something like joy in his heart. He ran about hunting for the fat yellow grains and finding them. First he ate all he could hold, for he saw that then there would be plenty to take home. Then he filled his cheeks full. He climbed up on the rail fence and started for his new home in the Old Orchard.

"It is a very long way to have to carry all my supplies," he thought, as he sat up on the top of a post to rest. "I don't see how I can ever do it. Well, I certainly can't, if I sit here all day!" With that he jumped down to the rail below him. He was halfway across when he noticed a crack in it. It looked to him as if that rail were empty inside part of the way. A great idea came to him. His eyes grew bright with excitement. He ran the length of the rail and back again, looking for an opening. There was none. Then very slowly and carefully he worked his way back. He stretched his head over so that he could look underneath. Almost over to the next post he found what he had hoped so much to find. What was it? Why, a knot-hole. Yes, Sir, a knot-hole that opened right into the empty space in the rail. It was not quite big enough for Chatterer to squeeze through, but that did not trouble him. He emptied the corn from his cheeks. Then he went to work with those sharp teeth of his. In a little while, a very little while, that knot-hole was plenty big enough for Chatterer to slip through.

His eyes snapped with pleasure as he explored the empty rail. "I'll make this my store-house!" he cried. "I'll fill it full of corn. Then when I am hungry in the winter, I can run down here and fill up. It will be a lot better than trying to carry the corn up to the Old Orchard." And with that, Chatterer began the work of filling the empty rail with corn.

*VIII*

*SAMMY JAY DROPS A HINT*

Whatever faults Chatterer the Red Squirrel may have, and they are many, laziness is not one of them. No, Sir, there is no laziness about Chatterer. When he has work to do, he does it. He keeps at it until it is finished. Every morning he got up with the sun. He raced along the old stone wall and the rail fences down to Farmer Brown's cornfield. There he first ate his breakfast. Then he worked to fill the empty rail of the fence which he had made into a store-house. It was hard work, because he had to do a great deal of hunting for the corn. And it was exciting work, because he had to keep his eyes and ears open every minute. He had to keep from furnishing a dinner for someone else.

Redtail the Hawk, who had not yet gone South, discovered him one morning. Chatterer hid behind a fence post just in time. After that, Redtail was there every morning. He watched from the top of a tree for Chatterer to grow careless and get too far from shelter.

Then one morning Reddy Fox surprised him at the edge of a heap of cornstalks. Chatterer had just time to twist his way to the middle of the heap. Reddy had seen him, and he could smell him. Very softly Reddy walked on tiptoe around the pile of cornstalks to see if Chatterer had come out on the other side. Then he came back to where Chatterer had gone in. He began to dig excitedly, making the dry stalks fly right and left. He made so much noise that Chatterer felt sure that he would not hear him move. And he did not. By the time Reddy had worked his way to the middle of the pile, Chatterer was safe in his store-house in the empty rail. He had slipped from under the cornstalks. He had run across to another pile. He had worked his way through this, and so reached the fence.

After that, Reddy Fox came every morning, hoping to surprise Chatterer. But Chatterer felt quite equal to fooling Reddy and Redtail. Of course they interfered with his work and were very troublesome. But he was not afraid of them. The one thing he did fear was that Shadow the Weasel would hear where he was. That thought troubled him a great deal.

One morning Sammy Jay just happened along. He saw Reddy Fox creeping up behind some bushes at the edge of the cornfield. At once Sammy began to scream, as he always does when he thinks he can spoil Reddy's hunting. Reddy looked up at him and showed all his long teeth. But Sammy only grinned and screamed the louder. Then Reddy walked away with a great deal of dignity. He knew that it was no use at all to try to hunt while Sammy Jay was about. When Reddy had disappeared in the Green Forest, Sammy returned to the cornfield. There he found Chatterer hard at work.

"I'm much obliged, Sammy, for driving that nuisance away," said Chatterer, as he hurried to the empty rail with his mouth full of corn. "He bothers me a great deal, and I've got to do a lot of work yet to fill my store-house before it is too late."

"Have you moved down here?" demanded Sammy Jay. "I thought you were living up in the Old Orchard."

"I am. At least, my house is up there, but there is no food there," replied Chatterer. "So I have made a store-house down here. I am trying to get it full of corn before snow comes."

"It will be a long way to come for your food every day," said Sammy.

"I know it," replied Chatterer, "but I guess I'm lucky to have any food to come for."

"Pooh!" said Sammy. "I wouldn't work as you do. I'd use my wits a little. If corn is what you want to eat, why don't you go up to Farmer Brown's? It's nearer to the Old Orchard than this. The corn is all stored ready for you to help yourself. I get all I want there."

*IX*

*CHATTERER SCREWS UP HIS COURAGE*

Ever since Sammy Jay had dropped a hint about the large supply of corn over at Farmer Brown's and how easy it was to get all that one wanted, Chatterer had been trying to gather his courage to go see for himself if Sammy had told the truth. Chatterer had spent most of his life in or close to the Green Forest. He had a very healthy fear of Farmer Brown's boy and his terrible gun. He always had been happy to keep away from Farmer Brown's door-yard. The truth is, he was afraid to go up there. You see, there were Black Pussy the Cat and Bowser the Hound and Farmer Brown's boy--why, it was a very dangerous place!

And yet Sammy Jay went up there every day and did not seem to be afraid at all. He even shouted and said rude things to Farmer Brown's boy. If Sammy dared go up there, why should not he? He certainly was as brave as Sammy Jay! Right down in his heart Chatterer had always thought Sammy Jay was a big coward. Yet here was Sammy going up there and helping himself to corn, just as if it belonged to him. Chatterer thought how hard he worked every day to fill that store-house in the hollow fence-rail. He thought how every minute of the time he had to watch out for Redtail the Hawk and Reddy Fox. It seemed as if he never, never could get enough corn to keep him all winter. And then it was a long way to go every day from the Old Orchard down to the cornfield. Chatterer sighed at the thought.

"If Sammy Jay told me the truth, and it is so easy to get all the corn one wants over there at Farmer Brown's, it will be much easier in bad weather," thought Chatterer. "Anyway, it will not do any harm to have a look and see for myself how things are."

So Chatterer started running quickly along the old stone wall which led right up to Farmer Brown's yard. As he drew near, he would stop every few steps to make sure that the way was clear. At last he reached the very end of the wall. He hid between two stones and looked out. Right across a wide road was Farmer Brown's house. In the sun on the back doorstep sat Black Pussy sleeping. Chatterer had hard work to hold his tongue. The very sight of her made him so angry that he almost forgot that he did not want to be seen. He just longed to tell her what he thought of her. But he kept still and set his sharp little eyes to discover where Farmer Brown kept his corn. He could see Bowser the Hound fast asleep in front of his own special little house. He could see the big barn and the henhouse and the shed where the wagons were kept and the long wood-shed.

"I wonder," said Chatterer to himself, "I wonder if that corn is kept in any of those places, and how Sammy Jay gets it if it is."

Just then Farmer Brown's boy came out of the barn. Chatterer moved back quickly at sight of him. He wanted to shout, just as he had wanted to shout at Black Pussy. But he wisely held his tongue. The very sight of it gathered his courage up till he felt brave enough to dare anything.

*X*

*CHATTERER STUDIES A WAY TO GET FARMER BROWN'S CORN*

Chatterer could think of but one thing--Farmer Brown's house full of corn, and how he could get some of it. Sammy Jay had said that he got all he wanted, and Chatterer made up his mind that he would see how Sammy did it.

Sammy looked this way and that way, without making the least sound. When he was quite sure that no one was about, he flew over to the strange little house on four legs, where Farmer Brown kept his corn. He pushed his bill in between the wide cracks of the wall. Chatterer grinned. "Sammy has taught me something, although he does not know it," said he to himself. "He's stealing that corn, and he does not think it safe to be found out. I must be just as careful as he is."

*XII*

*CHATTERER FRIGHTENS SAMMY JAY*

Chatterer the Red Squirrel was very pleased with himself because he had found a way of getting into Farmer Brown's corn-crib. So much beautiful yellow corn was stored there that it seemed to him there was enough for all the Squirrels in the world.

The more some people have, the more they want. It is the very worst kind of selfishness and is called greediness. Chatterer had found a way to get all the corn he wanted without working for it. *XIII*

*SAMMY JAY TELLS HIS TROUBLES TO REDDY FOX*

Sammy Jay could think of nothing but the terrible fright he had had at Farmer Brown's corn-crib. He had pushed his beak through a crack for a few grains of corn when something had seized his beak and held on. Sammy did not have the least bit of doubt that it was a trap of some kind set by Farmer Brown's boy. He flew down to the Green Forest to think it over and plan some way to get even with Farmer Brown's boy. As he sat there talking to himself, along came Reddy Fox. For a wonder Reddy saw Sammy before Sammy saw him.

Reddy grinned. "Sammy certainly has got something on his mind," thought Reddy. Then he said aloud: "Hello, Sammy! What is the matter? You look as if you had pain in your stomach and pain in your head and a few other pains."

"Enough to talk about, Reddy Fox! Enough to talk about!" snapped Sammy. Then, because he felt that he just had to tell someone, he told Reddy all about his terrible fright that morning.

"It was a trap," said Sammy. "It was some kind of a trap set by Farmer Brown's boy. Just as if he could not give a few grains of corn when he has got so much! I--I--I would like to--to peck his eyes out! That is what I would like to do!"

Sammy said that because it was the most terrible thing he could think of. But he did not really mean it. Reddy knew it and grinned. He did not believe that Farmer Brown's boy would ever set a trap in such a strange place as a crack in the wall of a corn-crib.

"He would not bother to try to trap Sammy Jay. He would just watch with his gun and shoot Sammy if he really cared about the few grains of corn Sammy has taken," thought Reddy. "It was some one or something else that scared Sammy. But it is not the least bit of use to tell him so. I believe I will have a look and see what is going on at that corn-crib." Aloud he said:

"What?" screamed Sammy. "Going up there again? What do you take me for? I guess I do not need but one lesson of that kind. "You are wise, very wise," replied Reddy seriously. "It is always wise to keep out of danger." And with this Reddy walked on up the Lone Little Path. Inside his red head were busy thoughts. And all day long Sammy Jay flew about through the Green Forest. He told every one who would listen how Farmer Brown's boy had tried to trap him. *XIV*

*REDDY FOX PLAYS SPY*

Reddy Fox did not have to get up early to be hiding behind the fence back of Farmer Brown's corn-crib when happy, round, red Mr. Sun chased the little stars from the sky. He did not have to get up early, for the very good reason that he had not been to bed. You see, Reddy Fox does a great many things that he would not like to have seen. So he does them in the night when most of the other little people of the Green Meadows and the Green Forest are asleep. And so it happens that often he does not go to bed at all at night. He sleeps in the day, when most honest people are out and about. He had been walking about all this night. Now he had come to watch and see what was going on at Farmer Brown's corn-crib. He wanted to see whether or not Farmer Brown's boy had been setting a trap there for Sammy Jay, as Sammy was so sure he had.

Just as the little stars disappeared and the first faint light from Mr. Sun began to chase away the black shadows, Reddy's sharp eyes saw something move over at the corner of the old stone wall at the edge of the Old Orchard. Reddy guessed right away that there was a hole there. He stood up and stretched.

*XV*

*SAMMY JAY SPOILS THE PLAN OF REDDY FOX*

*XVIII*

*CHATTERER HAS TO KEEP HIS PROMISE*

He could not think of anything else as he curled up for the night in his warm bed in the old home of Drummer the Woodpecker up in the Old Orchard. He would go over to the corn-crib before Sammy Jay was awake, eat his fill, and then hide from Sammy.

Anyway, he was sure that Sammy would not think of going to the corn-crib so late in the morning, for it would not be safe at all. Farmer Brown's boy would be almost sure to see him. *XIX*

*CHATTERER GETS SAMMY JAY SOME CORN*

This was bad enough in itself, but to make matters worse he had to do something which he felt was very dangerous. He had to get Sammy some corn from Farmer Brown's corn-crib right in broad daylight. He ran along the stone wall to the end at the edge of Farmer Brown's dooryard. Then he peeped out. Black Pussy was dozing on the doorstep. Her eyes were closed. Chatterer started across for the tree close by the corn-crib, and then his courage failed, and he ran back to the stone wall. Three times he did this, and each time he looked up to see Sammy Jay grinning at him from an apple-tree in the Old Orchard. It was very plain to see that Sammy was enjoying Chatterer's fright. Chatterer almost cried with fear and anger.

The fourth time he gritted his teeth and kept on running as fast as he knew how. He was almost past Black Pussy when she opened her eyes. In a flash she was after him. Chatterer reached the tree first and was up it like a little red streak. There he felt safe. At least, he felt safe from Black Pussy, for she would not dare follow him out on the small branches. But Farmer Brown's boy had seen her rush across to the foot of the tree, and now he stopped chopping wood to watch Black Pussy glaring up at Chatterer.

"What are you so interested in, Puss?" asked Farmer Brown's boy. He could not see Chatterer, because Chatterer was smart enough to keep on the other side of the tree trunk. "Is it something you want me to see?" he continued, and started to walk over to the tree.

Chatterer's heart was beating terribly with fright--thump, thump, thump! At just that minute there was a great racket over in the Old Orchard.

"Thief! thief! thief!" screamed Sammy Jay, making a great fuss. Farmer Brown's boy turned to look in that direction.

"I wonder if that fox is prowling around again," said he. And while he was still looking and wondering, Chatterer dropped to the roof of the corn-crib and slipped inside, through the hole he had found under the edge of the roof. He gave a great sigh of relief.

"I believe Sammy Jay did that purposely to make Farmer Brown's boy look over there instead of up in the tree," he muttered. And he was right. Sammy had no desire to have any real harm come to Chatterer, and so at just the right minute he had fooled Farmer Brown's boy, just as he often had fooled him before by screaming as if he saw Reddy Fox, when Reddy was not there at all.

When Farmer Brown's boy was sure that Reddy was not over in the Old Orchard, he once more turned to Black Pussy, who was still glaring up at the place where Chatterer had been. He looked up, too, but of course there was no one to be seen.

"I guess you must have dreamed you saw something, Puss," said he, stooping to stroke her gently. Then he went back to his wood-chopping. Black Pussy waited a few minutes longer and then went over to the barn to try to console herself with a mouse. Chatterer watched his chance and got back to the old stone wall safely, with his cheeks stuffed full of corn for Sammy Jay.

*XX*

*CHATTERER REMEMBERS SOMETHING*

Chatterer was disgusted with himself, with all his neighbors, and with the world in general, which is to say that Chatterer was very much put out about something. There was no doubt about it. He could not see anything cheerful in the sunshine nor anything pleasant in the blue, blue sky, and when any one fails to see cheerfulness in the sunshine or to find something pleasant in the blue, blue sky, there is something wrong in his own heart. That was the trouble with Chatterer. There was a great deal wrong in his heart.

In the first place, it was filled with anger, and anger, you know, will take all the joy and pleasantness out of anything. And then Chatterer was mortified. He was both angry and mortified because Sammy Jay had proved to have smarter wits than he had. So, as soon as he could do so without being seen, he slipped into his new home in the old house of Drummer the Woodpecker in an apple-tree in the Old Orchard, and there he sulked for the rest of the day. You see, Sammy Jay had made him go over to Farmer Brown's corn-crib and get him some corn right in broad daylight, and he had very narrowly escaped being seen by Farmer Brown's boy.

"If only I had not promised to get him corn whenever he asks me to!" he said over and over to himself. He was in a bad mood in his home in the apple-tree. "If only I had not! And yet I could not help myself--I just had to. Now whenever he feels like it, he will make me do as he did today. Perhaps I will not always be so lucky. Oh, dear; oh, dear; I have got myself into a terrible mess, and I have just got to think of some way out of it."

So all the rest of the day he thought and thought. The more he thought, the more sad he grew. It was not until just as he was going out for a breath of air before going to bed for the night that the great idea came to him.

"Stupid, stupid, stupid!" he said in a low voice, meaning himself. "Why did I not remember it before? You will not see me going over to that corn-crib again, Mr. Jay! I will get you the corn if I must, but you will not have the fun of laughing at me trying to run away from Black Pussy and Farmer Brown's boy. You are smart, Mr. Jay! You are smart, but you have got to get up early in the morning to play such a trick on Chatterer twice."

Right away he felt so much better in his mind. He had a quick run along the old stone wall and then went to bed for a good night's sleep. The next day Sammy Jay appeared in the middle of the morning and demanded more corn. Chatterer pretended that he did not dare go for it. But when Sammy insisted that he must, he suddenly started for--where do you think? Why, for that store-house of his in the hollow rail at the edge of the cornfield. It was a long way to go, but that was better than running the risk of being seen by Farmer Brown's boy. It took him some time, but at last he was back with his cheeks full of corn. Sammy Jay pretended to be angry because he had been kept waiting so long. He complained all the time he was eating. He pretended to think that the corn was not as good as that from Farmer Brown's corn-crib. He said in a low voice something about telling Shadow the Weasel if Chatterer did not get him some corn from the crib the next day.

"You cannot!" cried Chatterer in victory. "You promised not to tell Shadow if I kept my promise and got you corn whenever you asked for it. But I did not say where I would get it," and he laughed quietly to think that he had been smarter than Sammy Jay.

Sammy ate every grain and then went off. But as he went, Chatterer thought he heard something very like a quiet laugh. It made him think and feel a little worried. But he could not think of any way Sammy could get the best of him now, so he soon forgot it. All the rest of the day he thought of how lucky it was that he had remembered that store-house in the hollow rail.

*XXI*

*SAMMY JAY MAKES A CALL*

Sammy Jay had not had so much fun for a long time as he was having at the expense of Chatterer the Red Squirrel. No, Sir, Sammy had not had so much fun for as long as he could remember. You see, he and Chatterer never had been very good friends. They always had played sharp tricks on each other whenever they had the chance. Sammy had not forgotten how Chatterer had stolen the eggs of Drummer the Woodpecker in the spring. Then Chatterer had laid the blame on him, so that all the birds of the Old Orchard had driven him out until they discovered who the real thief was. Sammy had never forgotten or forgiven that sharp, mean trick. And now he was getting even. Right down in his heart he did not want any real harm to come to Chatterer, but he did love to see him scared. But his greatest fun was in matching his wits against those of Chatterer, for you know both have very sharp wits, as bad rascals are very likely to have.

Now all the time he had been saying in a low voice and finding fault with the corn Chatterer had brought from his storehouse in the hollow rail on the edge of the cornfield, Sammy had only been pretending. Yes, Sir, he had simply been pretending. You see, he had thought of that store-house before Chatterer had. He had thought Chatterer very stupid not to have remembered it in the first place. Now that Chatterer had remembered it, Sammy was glad, although he pretended not to be. Why was he glad? Well, you see, he knew that Chatterer was very pleased inside because he thought that he had proved himself smarter than Sammy. All the time Sammy saw another chance to prove to Chatterer that he was not so smart as he thought himself.

When he left Chatterer, he flew straight to the Green Forest and from there to the edge of the Green Meadows. His sharp eyes searched the Green Meadows until they saw his cousin, Blacky the Crow. Sammy flew straight over to where Blacky was sitting. For a few minutes they talked together. Then both looked over to a tall, lone tree out in the middle of the Green Meadows. In the top of it sat a black form very straight and very still. In fact, to eyes less sharp than those of Sammy Jay and Blacky the Crow, it would have looked very much like a part of the tree. It was Roughleg the Hawk watching for Danny Meadow Mouse.

[Illustration: Sammy flew straight over to where Blacky was sitting.]

"Will you do it?" asked Sammy. "I do not dare to myself because he might have a notion that a fat Jay like me would make him a good dinner."

"Of course I will do it," replied Blacky. "Old Roughleg never bothers me, and it will be a great joke."

"All right," replied Sammy. "Be on hand where you can see what happens tomorrow morning." And with that, Sammy Jay flew back to the Green Forest where he could watch.

In a few minutes Blacky the Crow flew over near the tree in which sat Roughleg the Hawk. Presently Sammy heard Blacky's harsh voice.

"Caw, caw, caw," said Blacky.

Sammy smiled. It was a signal, and he knew that Blacky had done as he had said he would. Then Sammy flew off to look for some new mischief with which to amuse himself for the rest of the day.

*XXII*

*CHATTERER HAS A TERRIBLE DAY*

Chatterer felt quite like himself again. He felt like his old bold, cheeky self as he looked out of his doorway at the first light of day. He felt that he had gotten the best of Sammy Jay the day before. To be sure, he had to get corn for Sammy. But he did not have to go to Farmer Brown's corn store for it. And he knew that Sammy wanted the fun of seeing him take that risk more than he wanted the corn. He felt that he had been smarter than Sammy. That feeling made him quite like his old self.

CHAPTER 5. Sammy Jay Makes a Fuss（5）

"Chickaro and chickaree, Who is there as smart as me? Chickaro and chickaree, Sharper minds you will never see."

Now that was bragging. Bragging is one of the most foolish habits in the world. But Chatterer always was a bragger and probably always will be. So he went quickly in and out of the old stone wall and said this over and over. He waited for Sammy Jay to appear. He had not gone over to Farmer Brown's corn store this morning for his breakfast. He felt sure that Sammy would come and send him for corn. He knew that he would have to go. But he meant to go down to his own store-house in the hollow rail on the edge of the cornfield. He could eat his fill there. So he ran about and wished that Sammy would hurry up. He was hungry.

At last Sammy came. Just as Chatterer expected, he demanded the corn that Chatterer had promised to get for him whenever he should ask for it. Right away Chatterer started for the cornfield. He ran along the fences. He always did like to run along fences. It was a long way down there, but he did not mind. It was a sharp, cold morning and the run made him feel fine. As he ran, he kept laughing quietly to himself. He thought how smart he had been to think of that store-house. He had found a way to keep his promise to Sammy Jay without any risk to himself. He was moving quickly along the fence on the edge of the cornfield. He had almost reached the hollow rail where he had stored the corn. He stopped to sit up on a fence-post and brag once more.

"Chickaro and chickaree! Who is there as smart--"

He did not finish. Instead his tongue seemed to stick to the roof of his mouth. His little black eyes looked as if they would pop out of his head. Sitting on a post close to the hollow rail was a straight, black form. It was watching him with cruel, hungry-looking eyes. It was Roughleg the Hawk! Chatterer gave a little gasp of fear. He turned around fast and started back along the fence as fast as his legs could go. Instantly Roughleg spread his great wings and flew after him. Chatterer had not gone the length of two rails before Roughleg was over him. With his great, cruel claws spread wide, he suddenly flew down fast. Chatterer moved quickly to the under side of the rail just in time. It was the very last moment. Roughleg screamed with disappointment. That scream had such a fierce sound that Chatterer shook all over.

[Illustration: Chatterer gave a little gasp of fear.]

How he ever got back to the Old Orchard he hardly knew himself. Many times he just managed to avoid those great claws. But he did get there at last. He was out of breath, tired and afraid. Sammy Jay sat there, waiting for his corn. He pretended to be very angry because Chatterer had none. He threatened to go straight to the Green Forest and tell Shadow the Weasel where Chatterer was living. Chatterer had nothing to do but to go over to the corn store as soon as he had rested a little.

"It's been a terrible day, a perfectly terrible day," said Chatterer to himself, as he curled up in bed for the night. "I wonder--I wonder how old Roughleg happened to be sitting on that fence-post this morning."

But Sammy Jay did not wonder. He knew.

*XXIII*

*CHATTERER HITS ON A PLAN AT LAST*

Each time that Chatterer thought himself smarter than Sammy Jay, he found that he was not as smart as he thought he was. This always made him feel ashamed. He just could not admit even to himself that Sammy was the smartest. And yet here he was every day bringing corn for Sammy from Farmer Brown's corn store whenever Sammy told him to. He was running the risk of being seen by Farmer Brown's boy. All this was because he had not been able to think of some way to outsmart Sammy. Once more, after he had such a narrow escape from old Roughleg the Hawk, he had tried going down to his store-house at the edge of the cornfield. But he had found Roughleg on watch and had turned back. Sammy Jay grinned when he saw Chatterer coming back. From that grin, Chatterer made up his mind that Sammy knew something. Sammy knew how old Roughleg happened to have found out about that store-house and so been on the watch.

Now all this time, Sammy Jay was having a great deal of fun out of Chatterer's trouble. Each time that Chatterer thought of a plan to outsmart Sammy, he would find that Sammy had already thought of it. Sammy also had thought of a way to make the plan quite useless. You see, Sammy used to spend a great deal of his time when he was alone in the Green Forest. He pretended that he was in the same fix as Chatterer. Then he tried to think of some way out of it. So it was that Chatterer never could think of a plan that Sammy had not already thought of. And yet there was a way to cheat Sammy out of his fun, though not out of his corn. It really was the fun of seeing Chatterer so worried that Sammy cared most about. Sammy had thought of it almost at once. It seemed to him that Chatterer was very, very stupid not to think of it, too.

"He will think of it some day, and I do not see any way to upset such a simple plan," said Sammy to himself. Then he fell to studying some new way to torment Chatterer.

And at last Chatterer did think of it. It was such a simple plan! Probably that was why he had not thought of it before. All he had to do was to go over to Farmer Brown's corn store at break of day. He had to go before any one in Farmer Brown's house was awake. He would do just as he had been doing. But he would make two or three trips and store a lot of corn in a safe hiding place in the old stone wall. Then, when Sammy Jay demanded corn, he could get it without trouble or danger. He tried it, and it worked very well. Sammy Jay got his corn, but he did not get any fun. He cared more for the fun of seeing Chatterer in trouble than he did for the corn. So, after two or three mornings, Sammy did not come up to the Old Orchard. Chatterer laughed quietly as he stored up the corn, not in one place, but in several places.

Now, Sammy Jay seemed to have grown tired of corn, but he was doing a lot of thinking. He had no idea of leaving Chatterer alone. He just had to think of some way to upset Chatterer's simple plan. It was Reddy Fox who finally gave him the idea. He saw Reddy running down the Lone Little Path through the Green Forest, and right away the idea came to him. He would tell Reddy where Chatterer was storing the corn in the old stone wall, and Reddy would hide close by.

"Of course I do not want Reddy to catch Chatterer, but I can prevent that by warning him just in time. But he will be so afraid that he will not dare go to that place for corn again soon, and so will have to go to the corn-crib for it," thought Sammy. He hurried to tell Reddy Fox about the place halfway along the old stone wall where Chatterer had hidden his corn.

*XXIV*

*CHATTERER HAS HIS TURN TO LAUGH*

Sammy Jay had not been up to the Old Orchard for several days, and Chatterer the Red Squirrel was beginning to wonder if Sammy had grown tired of corn. But Chatterer had learned that it is always best to be prepared. So every morning, when he had visited Farmer Brown's corn-crib, he had brought a generous supply back to the Old Orchard. He had hidden it in several secret places in different parts of the stone wall, and some in a certain hollow in an old apple-tree. Chatterer could not quite believe that Sammy had given up all hope of making him more trouble, so he meant to be prepared.

So when Sammy did appear early one morning, Chatterer was not surprised at all. He pretended to be glad to see Sammy. In fact, he was almost glad. You see, Sammy had so many times proved his wits to be sharper than Chatterer's, that Chatterer wanted to get even. There was a sparkle of mischief in Sammy's eyes. Chatterer saw it right away, and he guessed that Sammy had some new plan under that pert cap of his.

"Good morning, Sammy Jay," said Chatterer, pretending to be polite. "I had begun to think that you were tired of corn. I have some very nice corn ready for you, the very best I could find in Farmer Brown's corn-crib. Will you have some this morning?"

"I believe I will," replied Sammy, also pretending to be very polite. "It is very nice of you to pick out the best corn for me, and the very thought of it makes me hungry. I believe I would like some this very minute."

As he spoke, he turned his head to hide a grin. For, thought he, "of course Chatterer will go straight to that hiding place in the stone wall and then we shall see some fun." He glanced hastily in that direction, and he saw a patch of red half hidden behind the wall. He knew that it was the red coat of Reddy Fox. Reddy was hiding just where Sammy had told him to.

Now Chatterer had been doing some quick thinking. He remembered the sharp tricks Sammy had played on him before. He did not have the least doubt that Sammy had planned another. "Of course, he expects me to go straight to that place where he knows I have hidden corn for him. And if he has planned any trouble for me, that is where it will be," thought Chatterer. "I think I'll get the corn from one of the hiding places he does not know about."

With that Chatterer ran swiftly out along a branch of the tree he was in. He leaped across to another tree and then to a third, the one in which was the hollow in which he had put some of the corn. In a few minutes he was back, with his cheeks stuffed full. Sammy Jay pretended to be very much pleased, but he ate it as if he had lost his appetite, as indeed he had. You see, he was wondering what he should say to Reddy Fox, to whom he had promised a chance to catch Chatterer. He knew that Reddy would think that it was all one of Sammy's tricks. So without waiting to finish all the corn, Sammy politely said good-by and flew away to the deepest part of the Green Forest.

"Ha, ha, ha! Ho, ho, ho!" laughed Chatterer, as his sharp eyes spied Reddy Fox, trying to creep away without being seen. "Ha, ha, ha! Ho, ho, ho! It's my turn to laugh. Ha, ha, ha! Ho, ho, ho!"

And so for the time being Chatterer had the last laugh, though Sammy Jay knew well that his turn would come again, if only he were patient. But he had other things to think of. You see, he was very much interested in the adventures of Buster Bear. And if you are interested in them too, you may read all about them in another book devoted wholly to the things that happened when Buster came to live in the Green Forest.

THE END
