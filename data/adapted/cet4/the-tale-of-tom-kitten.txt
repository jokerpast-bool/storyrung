THE TALE OF TOM KITTEN

BY BEATRIX POTTER

Author of "The Tale of Peter Rabbit", and others.

FREDERICK WARNE

DEDICATED TO ALL PICKLES, ESPECIALLY TO THOSE THAT GET UPON MY GARDEN WALL.

Once, three little cats lived together. Their names were Mittens, Tom Kitten, and Moppet.

They had soft, white hair. They played in the dust on the front step.

One day, their mother, Mrs. Tabitha Twitchit, expected guests for tea. She brought the cats inside to wash them. She got them ready before the important people arrived.

First, she cleaned their faces. This one is Moppet.

Then, she brushed their hair. This one is Mittens.

Then, she brushed their tails. This is Tom Kitten.

Tom was very bad, and he moved a lot.

Mrs. Tabitha dressed Moppet and Mittens in clean shirts. Then, she took out stiff, hard to wear clothes. She wanted to dress her son, Thomas.

Tom Kitten was very fat. He had grown larger. Several buttons broke off his clothes. His mother fixed them back on.

When the three cats were ready, Mrs. Tabitha let them out into the garden. This was not wise. She did this so they would stay out of the way. She needed to make hot buttered bread.

"Now keep your clothes clean, children!" she said. "You must walk on your back legs. Stay away from the dirty fire pit. Also, stay away from Sally Henny Penny. And avoid the farm yard and the Puddle-Ducks."

Moppet and Mittens walked down the garden path. They walked in a shaky way. Soon, they stepped on their shirts. They fell on their noses.

When they stood up, they had green marks on their clothes.

"Let us climb up the rocks, and sit on the garden wall," said Moppet.

They turned their shirts back to front. They went up the rocks with a jump. Moppet's white hat fell down into the road.

Tom Kitten could not jump. He walked on his back legs. He wore pants. He climbed up the rocks slowly. He broke the plants. He dropped buttons left and right.

He fell into pieces when he reached the top of the wall.

Moppet and Mittens tried to pull him together. His hat fell off. The rest of his buttons burst open.

While they were in trouble, there was a sound. Pit pat paddle pat! The three Puddle-Ducks came along the hard road. They marched one behind the other. They did the march. Pit pat waddle pat!

They stopped and stood in a row. They looked up at the cats. They had very small eyes. They looked surprised.

Then, the two duck-birds, Rebeccah and Jemima Puddle-Duck, picked up the hat and hat. They put them on themselves.

Mittens laughed so hard. She fell off the wall. Moppet and Tom went down after her. The shirts came off. All of Tom's other clothes fell off.

"Come! Mr. Drake Puddle-Duck," said Moppet. "Come and help us to dress him! Come and button up Tom!"

Mr. Drake Puddle-Duck came forward slowly. He moved to the side. He picked up the different pieces of clothing.

But he put them on himself! They fit him even worse than Tom Kitten.

"It is a very fine morning!" said Mr. Drake Puddle-Duck.

And he and Jemima and Rebeccah Puddle-Duck set off up the road. They kept step with each other. Pit pat, paddle pat! Pit pat, waddle pat!

Then, Mrs. Tabitha Twitchit came down the garden. She found her cats on the wall. They had no clothes on.

She pulled them off the wall. She hit them. And she took them back to the house.

"My friends will arrive in a minute," said Mrs. Tabitha Twitchit. "You are not fit to be seen. I am very upset."

She sent them upstairs. And I am sorry to say this. She told her friends that the cats were in bed with the measles. Which was not true.

Quite the opposite. They were not in bed. Not in the least.

Somehow, there were very strange noises above. These sounds broke the quiet of the tea party.

And I think that some day I shall have to make another book. It will be larger. I will tell you more about Tom Kitten!

As for the Puddle-Ducks, they went into a pond.

The clothes all came off right away. Because there were no buttons.

And Mr. Drake Puddle-Duck, and Jemima and Rebeccah, have been looking for them ever since.
