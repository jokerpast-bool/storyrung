FOR THE CHILDREN OF SAWREY

FROM OLD MR. BUNNY

One morning a little rabbit sat on a bank.

He raised his ears and listened to the sound of a small horse walking.

A light carriage was coming along the road. Mr. McGregor was driving it, and Mrs. McGregor sat beside him in her best hat.

As soon as they had passed, little Benjamin Bunny slid down into the road. He set off with a jump and a run to visit his family, who lived in the wood behind Mr. McGregor's garden.

That wood was full of rabbit holes. In the tidiest, most sandy hole of all lived Benjamin's aunt and his cousins: Flopsy, Mopsy, Cotton-tail, and Peter.

Old Mrs. Rabbit was a woman whose husband had died. She earned her living by making mittens and wrist warmers from rabbit fur. I once bought a pair at a market. She also sold herbs, rosemary tea, and rabbit-tobacco, which is what we call lavender.

Little Benjamin did not very much want to see his Aunt.

He came around the back of the fir tree and almost fell on top of his Cousin Peter.

Peter was sitting by himself. He looked sick, and he was wearing a red cotton cloth.

"Peter," said little Benjamin, in a whisper, "who has got your clothes?"

Peter replied, "The scarecrow in Mr. McGregor's garden." He described how he had been chased around the garden and had dropped his shoes and coat.

Little Benjamin sat down beside his cousin. He told him that Mr. McGregor had gone out in a light carriage, and Mrs. McGregor too. He said they would surely be gone for the day, because she was wearing her best hat.

Peter said he hoped it would rain.

At this point old Mrs. Rabbit's voice came from inside the rabbit hole. She was calling: "Cotton-tail! Cotton-tail! go and get some more camomile!"

Peter said he thought he might feel better if he went for a walk.

They went away holding hands. They got up on the flat top of the wall at the bottom of the wood. From here they looked down into Mr. McGregor's garden. Peter's coat and shoes could be clearly seen on the scarecrow. On its head was an old soft cap of Mr. McGregor's.

Little Benjamin said: "It ruins people's clothes to squeeze under a gate. The right way to get in is to climb down a pear tree."

Peter fell down head first. But it did not matter, because the bed below had just been raked and was quite soft.

It had been planted with lettuces.

They left many strange little marks all over the bed, especially little Benjamin, who was wearing heavy wooden shoes.

Little Benjamin said that the first thing to do was to get back Peter's clothes, so that they could use the cloth.

They took them off the scarecrow. There had been rain during the night. There was water in the shoes, and the coat had shrunk a little.

Benjamin tried on the soft cap, but it was too big for him.

Then he suggested that they should fill the cloth with onions, as a little present for his Aunt.

Peter did not seem to be enjoying himself. He kept hearing noises.

Benjamin, on the other hand, felt completely at home, and ate a lettuce leaf. He said that he often came to the garden with his father to get lettuces for their Sunday dinner.

(The name of little Benjamin's papa was old Mr. Benjamin Bunny.)

The lettuces certainly were very fine.

Peter did not eat anything. He said he would like to go home. Soon he dropped half the onions.

Little Benjamin said that it was not possible to get back up the pear tree with a load of vegetables. He led the way bravely toward the other end of the garden. They went along a little path made of boards, under a sunny red brick wall.

The mice sat on their doorsteps cracking cherry stones. They winked at Peter Rabbit and little Benjamin Bunny.

Soon Peter let the cloth go again.

They got among flower pots, and frames, and tubs. Peter heard noises worse than ever. His eyes were as big as lolly-pops!

He was a step or two in front of his cousin when he suddenly stopped.

This is what those little rabbits saw around that corner!

Little Benjamin took one look. Then, in half a minute less than no time, he hid himself and Peter and the onions under a large basket....

The cat got up and stretched herself. She came and sniffed at the basket.

Perhaps she liked the smell of onions!

Anyway, she sat down on top of the basket.

She sat there for _five hours_.

* * * * *

I cannot draw you a picture of Peter and Benjamin under the basket, because it was quite dark, and because the smell of onions was terrible. It made Peter Rabbit and little Benjamin cry.

The sun went around behind the wood, and it was quite late in the afternoon. But still the cat sat on the basket.

At last there was a light tapping sound, and some bits of wall fell from above.

The cat looked up and saw old Mr. Benjamin Bunny walking proudly along the top of the wall of the upper terrace.

He was smoking a pipe of rabbit-tobacco, and he had a little stick in his hand.

He was looking for his son.

Old Mr. Bunny had no good opinion of cats at all.

He took a huge jump off the top of the wall onto the top of the cat. He hit it off the basket and kicked it into the greenhouse, tearing off a handful of fur.

The cat was too surprised to fight back.

When old Mr. Bunny had driven the cat into the greenhouse, he locked the door.

Then he came back to the basket and took out his son Benjamin by the ears. He hit him with the little stick.

Then he took out his nephew Peter.

Then he took out the cloth of onions, and walked out of the garden.

When Mr. McGregor returned about half an hour later, he noticed several things that puzzled him.

It looked as though someone had been walking all over the garden in a pair of heavy wooden shoes. But the marks were too ridiculously small!

Also he could not understand how the cat could have shut herself up _inside_ the greenhouse, locking the door from the _outside_.

When Peter got home, his mother forgave him, because she was so glad to see that he had found his shoes and coat. Cotton-tail and Peter folded up the cloth, and old Mrs. Rabbit tied up the onions and hung them from the kitchen ceiling, with the bunches of herbs and the rabbit-tobacco.
